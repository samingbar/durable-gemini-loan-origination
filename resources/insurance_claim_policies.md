# Insurance Claim Policy Guide

## Coverage Eligibility

- A claim is payable only when the policy status is `ACTIVE` or `IN FORCE`.
- Premium status must be `CURRENT`, `PAID`, or equivalent good standing at the time of loss.
- Coverage must be confirmed for the reported peril before a claim can be approved.
- A claim amount above the stated coverage limit is not payable without a documented endorsement.
- The claimant must exceed the deductible before a loss becomes payable.

## Documentation Requirements

- Property damage claims should include scene photos and at least one repair estimate.
- Receipts or proof of ownership should support contents claims above $2,500.
- A police report is required for theft, vandalism, and collision losses.
- Witness statements or medical notes should support any claim with injuries or third-party involvement.
- Missing essential documentation can justify a conditional decision or human review.

## Damages And Settlement

- Emergency mitigation costs can be reimbursed when invoices and loss details support the expense.
- A small gap between the claimed amount and the available estimate is normal.
- A gap above 25% between the claimed amount and the documented estimate requires additional scrutiny.
- Claims using more than 80% of available coverage should be escalated for senior review.
- Cleanly documented claims with prompt reporting can be approved without escalation.

## Fraud And Escalation Indicators

- Late reporting after 30 days is a fraud indicator and requires added scrutiny.
- Multiple prior claims within 3 years increase fraud and severity risk.
- Missing police reports, inconsistent item values, or thin documentation can require human review.
- A combination of late reporting, weak documentation, and high claim severity should be escalated.
- Injury claims and third-party disputes should be escalated when liability is unclear.

## Decision Thresholds

- Reject a claim when the policy is inactive, premium is not current, or coverage is not confirmed.
- Reject a claim when the claimed amount exceeds the coverage limit.
- Reject a claim when the claimed amount does not exceed the deductible.
- Use a conditional or human review decision for incomplete documentation, estimate mismatches above 25%, multiple prior claims, injuries, or third-party complexity.
- Approve claims that are covered, timely reported, well documented, and financially consistent with the evidence.
